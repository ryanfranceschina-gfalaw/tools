Innocent Defense

GFA LAW calls its criminal defense work Innocent Defense. We are trial attorneys, and we prepare every case as if it will go before a jury. That means testing the State's evidence, challenging unlawful stops and searches, and holding the government to its burden of proof beyond a reasonable doubt. GFA LAW defends clients in Temple, Bell County and courts across Texas.

Courts We Appear In

  • Municipal and Justice of the Peace courts (Class C offenses)
  • County courts at law (Class A and B misdemeanors)
  • District courts (felonies)
  • Appeals and post-conviction matters

How We Approach a Case

Every case starts with the evidence: body camera and dash camera footage, reports, lab results and witness statements. We look at whether officers had a lawful reason to stop, detain, search or arrest. Where the facts support it, we file motions to suppress evidence and we take the case to trial.

If You Have Been Arrested

  • Stay calm and do not resist, even if you believe the arrest is unlawful
  • Say clearly that you will remain silent and that you want a lawyer
  • Do not consent to searches of your car, phone or home
  • Write down names, badge numbers, times and witnesses as soon as you can
  • Contact a lawyer before talking with investigators or prosecutors

What Innocent Defense Means for You

Many criminal cases in Texas end in plea bargains, often before anyone has looked closely at the evidence. Innocent Defense starts from the presumption of innocence that the Constitution guarantees. We investigate the case ourselves, we do not assume the police report is accurate, and we make the State prove every element of the charge. A plea offer may still come, and when it does we explain it clearly so you can decide. The decision whether to accept a plea or go to trial is always yours.

Charges We Defend

  • Class C misdemeanors in municipal and justice courts, including citations that can lead to arrest records
  • Class A and B misdemeanors in county courts at law
  • State jail and felony charges in district court
  • Weapons charges, including cases arising from lawful carry
  • Charges that follow protests, public meetings or recording police, such as interference, resisting or disorderly conduct

Evidence and Discovery

Under the Michael Morton Act (Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Article 39.14), the State must produce the evidence in its possession that is material to the case, including information that tends to show innocence. We request and review all of it: offense reports, body camera and dash camera video, 911 recordings, lab reports and witness statements. When officers stopped, searched or arrested someone without legal justification, Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Article 38.23 and the Fourth Amendment can require the evidence to be excluded.

Bell County and Beyond

Our office is in Temple, and we appear in courts throughout Bell County. We also represent clients in courts across Texas. Wherever your case is pending, we will tell you up front whether travel affects the fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer for a Class C ticket?

A Class C offense carries a fine, not jail, but a conviction still creates a record. Some Class C charges can also be the start of a larger case. A short consultation can tell you whether it is worth contesting.

Will my case go to trial?

We prepare every case for trial, because thorough preparation gives you the strongest footing at every stage. Whether your case actually goes to trial is your decision.

Should I talk to the police to clear things up?

Not without a lawyer. You have the right to remain silent and the right to counsel. Say clearly that you are invoking both, and then stop talking.
