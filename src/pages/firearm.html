Second Amendment

Firearm Rights and Expunction

The Second Amendment protects the right to keep and bear arms, and Texas law gives gun owners specific tools to protect that right. GFA LAW represents gun owners in Temple, Bell County and across Texas in criminal cases involving firearms, in efforts to recover seized firearms, and in clearing records of past weapons arrests.

Unlawful Carry and Weapons Charges

Since September 1, 2021, most adults 21 and older who can legally possess a handgun may carry one in Texas without a license. Officers still make arrests for unlawful carry, places where weapons are prohibited, and possession by a prohibited person. We examine whether the stop and search were lawful, whether the location was properly posted, and whether the State can prove each element of the offense.

Getting Seized Firearms Back

When police seize a firearm, Texas law sets out a process for its return. Under Article 18.19 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, an owner who is not prohibited from possessing a firearm may ask for its return after a case is dismissed or ends without a conviction, and the court can order it released. Agencies often hold firearms until someone files the right request, so acting promptly matters.

Expunction of Past Unlawful Carry Arrests

House Bill 1927 created a path to expunge certain unlawful carry arrests and convictions for conduct that would be legal under the current permitless carry law. Other arrests may qualify for expunction or an order of nondisclosure depending on how the case ended. We review your records and tell you which option, if any, fits your history.

Restoring Federal Firearm Rights

Federal law bars many people from having firearms, including anyone with a felony conviction or a misdemeanor domestic violence conviction. As of November 4, 2026, the Department of Justice accepts applications to restore federal firearm rights under 18 U.S.C. 925(c). The program covers every federal firearm disability, relief is discretionary, and a grant does not by itself change your rights under Texas law. GFA LAW prepares these applications. Answer four screening questions on our Restore My Rights page to see whether we can take yours.

Expunction or Nondisclosure?

An expunction removes an arrest from official records. Once it is granted, agencies must destroy or return the records, and in most situations you may deny that the arrest occurred. An order of nondisclosure seals a record from the public, but law enforcement and certain licensing agencies can still see it. Eligibility for each depends on the offense, how the case ended and your other history, so we review your full record before recommending a path.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will police return my gun automatically when my case is dismissed?

Usually not. Agencies often keep a seized firearm until the owner makes a proper request or a court orders its release. If the law's notice and claim deadlines pass, a firearm can be forfeited, sold or destroyed.

Can I get my gun back while charges are still pending?

Generally, a firearm held as evidence stays in custody while the case is open. We can still prepare the request so it is ready when the case ends.

How long does an expunction take?

It depends on the court and on how quickly agencies respond, and it often takes several months from filing to final order. After the order is signed, agencies need additional time to update their records.

Does an old unlawful carry arrest still matter?

It can show up on background checks and applications. If the conduct would be legal under the current permitless carry law, it may qualify for expunction.
