Coryell County Civil Rights and Criminal Defense

Coryell County borders Bell County to the west and shares Fort Hood with it. We represent people in Gatesville, Copperas Cove and the rest of the county in civil rights lawsuits, criminal cases and firearm rights matters.

Criminal cases in Coryell County

Felony cases in Coryell County are heard in the state district courts, including the 52nd and 440th District Courts. Most misdemeanor cases are heard in the County Court at Law of Coryell County. Class C misdemeanors, such as many traffic and fine-only offenses, are handled in justice of the peace and municipal courts. Coryell County courts sit in Gatesville, the county seat.

  • The District Attorney for the 52nd Judicial District prosecutes felonies.
  • The Coryell County Attorney's Office handles misdemeanor prosecutions.

GFA LAW practices Innocent Defense. Our approach is to prepare each criminal case as if it will go to trial, investigate the facts, and never assume the police report is the whole story. Learn about Innocent Defense.

Civil rights lawsuits

When an officer or official in Coryell County violates your constitutional rights, a lawsuit under 42 U.S.C. 1983 can hold them accountable. These cases are usually filed in federal court. Cases arising in Coryell County belong to the Waco Division of the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas, whose clerk's office is at 800 Franklin Avenue in Waco.

The agencies people in Coryell County most often deal with include the Coryell County Sheriff's Office, Gatesville Police Department, Copperas Cove Police Department, and the Texas Department of Public Safety. We handle claims for unlawful arrest, excessive force, illegal searches, and retaliation for speech, filming police or carrying a firearm. Learn about Section 1983 lawsuits.

Clearing your record

Most expunction petitions are filed in district court, through the Coryell County District Clerk. Some cases, such as acquittals and certain Class C matters, can be handled by the trial court. An order of nondisclosure for a case that ended in deferred adjudication goes back to the court that granted the deferred adjudication: the district court for a felony or the county court at law for a misdemeanor. Eligibility depends on the offense, how the case ended and your other history. Check your eligibility.

Firearm rights

If police in Coryell County seized your firearm, Texas law gives you a way to ask for it back once the case ends. People barred from firearms by federal law can now apply to the Department of Justice to restore their federal rights. Learn about federal firearm rights restoration.

Fort Hood

Soldiers, veterans and military families from Fort Hood (known as Fort Cavazos from 2023 to 2025) often face civilian charges or police encounters off post in Coryell County. A civilian arrest can carry consequences beyond the criminal case itself, so it is worth getting advice early. We handle the civilian side: the criminal case, the arrest record, any seized firearms, and civil rights claims when officers cross the line.
