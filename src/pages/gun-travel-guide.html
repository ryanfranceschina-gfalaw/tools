    Traveling with a gun: common questions

    What is concealed carry reciprocity?

    Reciprocity is when one state honors a carry license issued by another state, by written agreement or by its own statute. Some states honor every state’s license, some honor only a list, some honor only licenses issued to residents of the issuing state, and some honor none. Many states also allow permitless (constitutional) carry, so a visitor may not need a license there at all. Use the map above to see how your license is treated in each state on your route.

    Can I drive through a state that does not honor my permit?

    Federal law (18 U.S.C. 926A) protects you while traveling if you may lawfully have the firearm where your trip starts and where it ends. The firearm must be unloaded, and the firearm and ammunition must not be readily accessible from the passenger area. If the vehicle has no trunk, use a locked container other than the glove box or console. Stopping overnight or for errands in a strict state can put you outside that protection.

    How do I fly with a gun?

    Under TSA rules, firearms travel only in checked baggage, unloaded, in a locked hard-sided case, and you must declare them at the airline counter every time. Ammunition must be in a box made for ammunition, and magazines must be boxed or in the locked case. The law of the state where you land and claim your bag still applies.

    Can I take a gun on Amtrak?

    Yes, as checked baggage only, on trains and at stations that offer checked baggage. Reserve by calling 1-800-USA-RAIL at least 6 hours before departure. The firearm must be unloaded in a locked hard-sided case (up to 62 x 17 x 7 inches and 50 pounds), with up to 11 pounds of ammunition, and you check in at least 30 minutes early.

    Can I take a gun on a Greyhound bus?

    No. Greyhound and FlixBus do not allow firearms, ammunition or gun parts in carry-on or checked bags.

    Can I carry a gun in a national park?

    Federal law lets you possess a firearm in a national park if it is legal under the law of the state where the park is located (54 U.S.C. 104906). Federal buildings in the park, such as visitor centers, remain off limits.

    General information, not legal advice. Gun laws change often and cities can add their own rules.
