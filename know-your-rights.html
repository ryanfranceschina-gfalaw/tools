Know Your Rights
When Police Stop You

A pocket guide for Texas and Maryland from GFA LAW, PLLC. Keep it in your glovebox and share it with family.

1Stay calm, stay safe

  • Keep your hands where officers can see them. Do not run, argue or physically resist, even if you believe the stop is wrong. You fight it later, in court.
  • Ask clearly:
    “Officer, am I being detained, or am I free to go?”
    If you are free to go, leave calmly.

2Use your right to remain silent

  • You do not have to answer questions about where you are going, what you did, or whether you have anything illegal.
  • Say it out loud, then stop talking:
    “I am going to remain silent. I want a lawyer.”
  • Do not lie to police. Silence is legal; false statements are not.

3Identification

  • Drivers must show a driver’s license when an officer asks (both states). Texas drivers must also show proof of insurance.
  • Texas drivers: if you are stopped and cannot show a license, you must give your name, license number, address and date of birth when asked (Tex. Penal Code § 38.02(b-1)).
  • Texas, on foot: you only have to give your name, address and date of birth if you are lawfully arrested, but giving a false name or date of birth while detained is a crime (§ 38.02).
  • Maryland, on foot: there is no general “stop and identify” law, but never give false information.

4Searches

  • Officers may ask to search your car, phone, bag or home. You can say no:
    “I do not consent to any searches.”
  • Do not block or physically stop a search. If officers search anyway, your lawyer can challenge it later.
  • Do not unlock your phone or give passcodes.

5Recording the police

  • You may record officers doing their jobs in public places in Texas and Maryland.
  • Keep a reasonable distance, do not interfere, and follow lawful orders. Keep your phone visible and say you are recording.
  • Back up the video right away, before you hand your phone to anyone.

6If you are carrying a handgun

  • Tell the officer calmly before you reach for anything, and keep your hands visible.
  • Texas: most adults 21 and older may carry without a license. If you hold a License to Carry and an officer asks for ID, show your LTC with your ID (Tex. Gov’t Code § 411.205).
  • Maryland: carrying a handgun requires a Wear and Carry permit, and you must have it with you whenever you carry.

7Afterward: protect your case

  • Write down officer names, badge and car numbers, the time and place, and any witnesses.
  • Save every video, photo, text and receipt. Ask witnesses to save theirs.
  • Get medical care for any injury and keep the records.
  • Do not post about it online or talk about it with anyone but your lawyer.
  • Keep every ticket, bond paper, property receipt and court notice.
  • Deadlines are short. Injury and property claims against Texas cities and counties can require written notice within 6 months, and some Texas city charters set shorter deadlines. Maryland local governments require written notice within 1 year.
