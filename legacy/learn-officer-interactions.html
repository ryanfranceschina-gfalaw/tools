Was Your Interaction Lawful?

A substantive guide to help you determine if the police overstepped their constitutional boundaries.

Reasonable Suspicion Probable Cause
Reasonable Suspicion: The "Investigative" Standard
Standard: Terry v. Ohio
🚶 Pedestrian Detentions

To stop you, an officer must point to "specific and articulable facts" suggesting that criminal activity is occurring or about to occur. A mere "hunch" or a person's presence in a high-crime area is not enough. While certain behaviors can contribute to Reasonable Suspicion, the officer must be able to explain exactly what they saw that was suspicious.

🚗 Traffic Stops

A stop is lawful only if the officer has a reasonable belief that a traffic violation has occurred. However, the stop must stay focused on that violation. If an officer extends the stop to wait for a K-9 unit or to ask unrelated questions without new Reasonable Suspicion, the detention may become unlawful.

Identify Potential Misconduct

  • Lack of Justification: Did the officer fail to tell you why you were stopped, or did their reason feel like a fabrication?
  • Illegal Frisk: Did they reach into your pockets? Under this standard, they are only allowed a "pat-down" of outer clothing for weapons.
  • Extended Detention: Were you held for an unreasonable amount of time after the initial reason for the stop was resolved?
Probable Cause: The "Search & Arrest" Standard
Standard: 4th Amendment
🚶 Search of Person or Home

This is a high burden. It requires a "fair probability" that a crime was committed or that evidence exists in a specific place. To search your person or home without a warrant, the police must prove an emergency exists or have your voluntary (un-coerced) consent.

🚗 Search of a Vehicle

Under the Automobile Exception, police can search your car without a warrant only if they have Probable Cause—such as seeing contraband in plain view or smelling a substance. Without this, they generally cannot search your trunk or closed containers without your permission.

Identify Potential Misconduct

  • Coerced Consent: Did you feel "forced" to agree to a search because of threats or aggressive behavior?
  • Illegal Entry: Did officers enter your home without a warrant or a clear, life-threatening emergency?
  • Arrest Without Facts: Were you arrested based on an officer’s "gut feeling" rather than objective evidence of a crime?

If your interaction with police does not align with these standards, your constitutional rights may have been violated.
